# The Core Equation

$$O = (N^2) \rightarrow [\text{UNS}] \rightarrow O = (N) \wedge I4^2$$

The equation is the [[Industrial Field Standard]] in its irreducible form. It identifies the problem condition, the success condition, and the catalyst that crosses between them. This note serves as the navigation index — clicking any term reaches its concept note; each chapter heading reaches the corresponding Foundations chapter.

The equation organises itself into six structural parts, each of which corresponds to a chapter in [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS|Theoretical Foundations]] and a body of established science that the framework subsumes.

---

## 1. The Sequential Constraint — `O = (N²)`

The problem condition. The state in which integration cost scales quadratically because every new node must maintain direct connections to every existing node it communicates with.

**Concept primitive:** [[Quadratic Entanglement]]
**Axiom:** [[Axiom I — Quadratic Entanglement]]
**Foundations chapter:** [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^chapter-1-summary|§1 — The Sequential Constraint]]

**Laws that map here:**
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-1-brooks|Brooks's Law (1975)]] — N(N−1)/2 interface arithmetic
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-2-conway|Conway's Law (1968)]] — communication structure mirrors integration topology
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-3-ashby|Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety (1956)]] — flat architectures cannot represent multi-dimensional reality
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-4-forrester|Forrester's System Dynamics (1961)]] — structure determines behaviour, not effort
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-5-amdahl|Amdahl's Law (1967)]] — sequential portions impose a hard performance ceiling
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-6-shannon|Shannon's Channel Capacity Theorem (1948)]] — translation layers as noisy channels
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-7-barabasi|Barabási-Albert Preferential Attachment (1999)]] — hub-and-spoke pathology
- [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^law-1-8-dunbar|Dunbar's Number (1992/1993)]] — cognitive limits on sequential coordination

## Reading the equation as the framework's coordinate system

Every claim the framework makes locates itself somewhere in this equation. Every law of established science that the framework subsumes maps to one of the six parts. Every concept primitive the framework introduces is positioned at one or more of these structural locations.

The equation is the framework's coordinate system in the same way that ISA-95 is the coordinate system the [[Unified Namespace]] operates within: a single, structurally-grounded address space that every other element of the framework locates itself relative to. To know where a claim sits in the framework is to know which part of the equation it serves.

This note is the navigation index for that coordinate system.

---

## Foundational posture

The equation is articulated, not argued. The framework's posture toward established science is one of subsumption: the laws above were not the framework's theoretical starting points. They were discovered when the equation was validated against the existing body of established science. See [[THEORETICAL_FOUNDATIONS#^discovered-not-declared-register|the Foundational Posture chapter]] for the formal account of this register.
